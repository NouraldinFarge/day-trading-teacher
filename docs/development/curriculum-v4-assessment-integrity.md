# Curriculum 4.0 assessment-integrity response

This document records the implementation response to the supplied 90/100
curriculum audit. The audit found a strong process-first sequence and unusually
good evidence discipline, but identified predictable answer placement,
participation-style mastery language, repeated imported content, oversized
capstones, and a missing current account-and-regulation unit. Curriculum 4.0
addresses those gaps without turning the application into a signal service or
claiming that lesson completion predicts profitability.

## System-wide changes

- The nine core lessons contain 72 purposeful activities and 27 objective
  checks. Authored correct answers are balanced 7/7/7/6 across positions zero
  through three. The UI applies another deterministic shuffle for every new
  attempt while preserving the order across a resumable session.
- The included plan contains eight advanced extension lessons, 64 activities,
  and 24 checks. Its authored correct answers are balanced exactly 6/6/6/6.
- Distractors model realistic errors: using an effective date without checking
  a firm's phase-in, treating a later quote as execution-time evidence,
  estimating size before structural invalidation, or assuming a price boundary
  removes gap exposure. Cartoon errors such as deleting records or ignoring
  risk were removed.
- Every lesson declares an observable standard: at least two of three checks
  correct before feedback, a specified number of new outcome-hidden cases, a
  minimum number of successful cases, level 2 or better on every analytic-rubric
  dimension, separated retention dates, and an exact remediation route.
- The common rubric is Evidence accuracy, Decision accuracy, Transfer, and
  Explanation, each scored 0 Not demonstrated, 1 Guided, 2 Independent, or 3
  Transfer-ready. A completion can record honest guided practice without
  falsely advancing the mastery artifact.
- Long planning and capstone lessons publish bounded session blocks. Capstones
  label activities as instruction, independent performance, or review so a
  worked example cannot be counted as independent evidence.

## Core lesson-by-lesson critique and response

### 1. Reconstruct before you judge

The foundation was already strong, but clean timelines could hide evidence
conflicts. Version 4 keeps partial fills, weighted-average entry, cancellations,
unmatched positions, and source authority, then adds conflicting clocks and a
five-case independent standard. The learner must preserve uncertainty rather
than choose the timestamp that makes the trade look coherent.

### 2. Know the account before the setup

This is the new missing unit. It separates settled cash from unsettled proceeds,
requires dated verification of the firm's current intraday-margin and house
requirements, prices activity after friction, establishes a suitability stop,
and explicitly assesses broker-specific fractional eligibility, sessions,
order types, aggregation, timing, and residual-share behavior. Account facts
are an eligibility boundary, not a strategy recommendation.

### 3. Set the loss boundary first

The quantitative core remains correct and conservative. The revised distractors
now test whether a learner omits adverse slippage, nets correlated exposure
without a written policy, or estimates quantity before defining structural
invalidation. Five new cases and four successful rubric passes replace the
former broad mastery language.

### 4. Make the setup earn eligibility

The strongest feature remains permission to wait or reject. Transfer now
requires changed volatility and liquidity conditions, while plausible
distractors test stale thresholds and outcome leakage. A passing gate never
requires a trade.

### 5. Write the decision before the ticket

The content was too large for one unbroken sitting. It is now presented as
three blocks: architecture instruction, independent plan construction, and
review/remediation. Six unseen plans, five successful plans, and the common
rubric make “auditable by another person” measurable.

### 6. Choose the order by its failure mode

This lesson now declares Investor.gov's order-type reference and uses realistic
confusions between execution priority, price boundaries, stop triggers, and
unfilled stop-limit exposure. It never labels one order universally safer and
requires independent comparison of changing spread, urgency, and no-fill cost.

### 7. Make the next decision independent

The reset remains outcome-neutral. Revised alternatives test fixed cooldowns
that override observable rules and stale triggers that feel recent. Transfer
cases cover wins, losses, misses, partial fills, and rapid reappearance without
rewarding trade frequency.

### 8. Score process and preserve uncertainty

The two-axis review remains a major strength. Mastery now requires scoring
ambiguous winners and losses, keeping unsupported dimensions not scorable, and
routing one correction that can be tested on a later case. P&L never supplies
missing process evidence.

### 9. Run the complete no-click replay

The capstone is now a 48-minute, three-session assessment: calibration,
independent performance, and review. It requires six fixed-reveal cases with at
least five rubric successes and preserves eligible wait or no-trade decisions.
The original artifacts stay locked; remediation routes to the lowest dimension
instead of repeating cases until the win rate looks better.

## Included plan: duplicate-to-extension conversion

| Included lesson | Core prerequisite | Deliberate extension |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Build the evidence clock | Reconstruct before you judge | Conflicting clocks, missing intent, incomplete positions, and six unfamiliar records |
| Price the market-order decision | Choose the order by its failure mode | Changing spread, liquidity, partial fills, dollar orders, and no-fill tradeoffs |
| Turn a fast mover into an eligibility test | Make the setup earn eligibility | Passing, borderline, and failing gates across changing regimes |
| Make every re-entry earn independence | Make the next decision independent | Resets after wins, losses, misses, partial fills, and fast reappearance |
| Say the exit before the entry | Write the decision before the ticket | Structural, objective, time, gap, halt, partial-fill, and cancellation contingencies |
| Review the decision, not the story | Score process and preserve uncertainty | Ambiguous outcomes, not-scorable evidence, analytic scoring, and routed correction |
| Build a session stopping rule | Make the next decision independent | Earliest-of risk, attention, and process boundaries without a profit quota |
| Run a no-click replay lab | Run the complete no-click replay | Harder six-case assessment with incomplete evidence and changed regimes |

Every extension requires six outcome-hidden cases, at least five successful
cases, level 2 across the common rubric, and two separated standard-qualified
practice dates. The imported plan therefore serves overlearning and transfer,
not repeated introductory exposure.

## Current-source and product boundaries

The account unit's factual boundary was checked on 2026-07-22 against
[FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-10](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10),
[FINRA's frequent intraday trading guidance](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/frequent-intraday-trading),
[FINRA's fractional-share guidance](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/investing-fractional-shares),
and [Investor.gov's order-type guide](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/how-stock-markets-work/types-orders).
The app tells the learner to verify the specific firm's dated implementation and
house requirements; it does not treat regulatory summaries as account-specific
advice.

Remaining limits are intentional. Case counts and rubric scores are local
self-reports, not proctored certification. Historical bars cannot reconstruct
intrabar ordering or execution quality. Tax questions remain questions for a
qualified professional. Fidelity Trader+ remains a user-controlled companion:
the app does not access credentials, scrape the screen, or place orders.
